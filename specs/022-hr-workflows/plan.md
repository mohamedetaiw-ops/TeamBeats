R2 builds the rest of the spec: leave, reduced hours, team availability, attendance, onboarding and offboarding journeys, employee data change, HR letters and expense claims. The R2 plan is at the end of this file. Probation and contract milestones, shift rosters and AI receipt reading for all tenants stay in R3.

## Where a calendar applies (spec gap, smallest safe default)
The spec says "per country and location", but the hierarchy of spec 002 has no location entity. Default: a calendar is assigned to an org unit and applies to that unit's subtree; the nearest assigned ancestor wins. A person's calendar is the one that applies to their primary membership unit (spec 002). With no assignment, the tenant calendar applies (Monday to Friday, no holidays, US2 scenario 4). A country is a label on the calendar, not a separate entity. Recorded in the R1 implementation notes; a location entity, if spec 010 or 022 adds one later, maps onto the same assignment.

## Data model (schema `hr`)
- `working_calendar`: `tenant_id`, `id`, `name` (localized jsonb), `country` (ISO 3166 alpha-2, optional), `week_days smallint[]` (ISO day numbers), `week_start`, `is_tenant_default`, `version`, timestamps. Exactly one tenant default per tenant (partial unique index).
- `calendar_holiday`: `tenant_id`, `calendar_id`, `date`, `name` (localized), `half_day bool`. Unique per calendar and date.
- `calendar_assignment`: `tenant_id`, `org_unit_id` (unique), `calendar_id`.
- Seed on first use: a tenant default calendar Monday to Friday.

## Contract (`Hr.Contracts`)
- `IWorkingCalendar.ForUnitAsync(orgUnitId)` and `ForUserAsync(userId)` return a resolved calendar snapshot.
- `IsWorkingDay(date)`, `CountWorkingDays(from, to)`, `AddWorkingDays(start, n)` (in the tenant time zone of spec 001), pure functions over the snapshot, unit tested.
- Snapshot cached in memory per tenant and calendar version; invalidated by `WorkingCalendarChanged`.

## API (v1)
- `GET/POST /api/v1/hr/calendars`, `GET/PUT/DELETE /api/v1/hr/calendars/{id}` with `If-Match` versions.
- `PUT /api/v1/hr/calendars/{id}/holidays` (replace the holiday list for a year), `PUT /api/v1/hr/assignments/{orgUnitId}`.
- `GET /api/v1/hr/working-days?orgUnitId=&from=&to=` for admin preview.
- Validation keys `validation.calendar.*`; the default calendar cannot be deleted; a calendar in use cannot be deleted until unassigned.

## Events and audit
- `WorkingCalendarChanged(calendarId, affectedDates)` on any change. Specs 016 and 024 recalculate open due times and timers from the next evaluation (US2 scenario 2).
- Audit `hr.calendar_changed` with calendar, dates and actor.

## UX
- Admin shell section "Calendars": list, edit form (name in each enabled locale, country, working week picker, holiday table with add, edit and remove), assignment to units with the org tree picker, and a working-day preview. Gregorian dates, optional Hijri display per spec 007. Keyboard and screen reader use as a table.

## Tests
- Unit: working-day arithmetic across weekends, holidays, half days, Sunday to Thursday weeks, year ends, time zones.
- Integration: US2 scenarios 1, 2 (event published, R1 part) and 4; nearest assigned ancestor wins; HR officer of a sibling unit is denied; cross-tenant calendar and unit IDs are not found; audit asserted.
- Acceptance (e2e): create a calendar, add holidays and assign it, in English and Arabic, with no critical axe findings.

## Gaps and smallest safe defaults (R2)
- **Location**: as in R1, a calendar and a leave policy apply to an org unit subtree; the nearest assigned ancestor wins.
- **Balances owner**: TeamBeats keeps balances (open question default) unless the tenant's HR connection owns them (slice 2).
- **Accrual**: monthly, on the first day of the month in the tenant time zone, `entitlement / 12` per month; a yearly grant is the other choice. Service years and grade rules pick the entitlement from the first matching band.
- **Carry-over**: at the start of the leave year (1 January unless the type sets another month), the unused balance up to the type's carry-over limit moves to the new year; the rest lapses, in the ledger.
- **Leave year of a request**: each day counts against the year it falls in (edge case "across a year end").
- **Half days**: a request may start or end on a half day; hours only when the type allows hours.
- **Sensitive types**: shown to colleagues as "out of office" only (section 6); the approver and HR officers see the type.
- **Replacement**: an accept task in the inbox; a decline notifies the requester, who can name someone else. Approval does not wait for the replacement.
- **Profile status**: People shows "out of office" from `LeaveApproved` and `LeaveCancelled` (spec 010 profile status), dates only.
- **Pre-boarding**: on, behind a tenant setting, off by default until the HR officer turns it on (open question default "yes, tenant setting").
- **Letter verification**: on by default, the tenant can turn it off (open question default).
- **Performance management**: not planned (open question default).

## Data model (schema `hr`, slice 1)
- `leave_type`: id, key, names (localized), unit (`day` or `hour`), half days allowed, paid, sensitive, needs document after N days, notice days, negative allowed and its maximum, eligibility (membership types, gender when enabled), carry-over limit, year start month, cancel needs approval, active, version.
- `leave_policy`: id, leave type, org unit (null for the tenant), entitlement bands (by service years and grade), accrual (`monthly` or `yearly`), version. The nearest ancestor policy wins, like calendars.
- `leave_ledger`: one row per change to a person's balance: person, type, leave year, delta, reason (`accrual`, `carry_over`, `lapse`, `request`, `cancel`, `adjustment`, `recount`), request, actor, at. The balance is the sum; it is never stored on its own (section 14, balance tampering).
- `leave_request`: id, number, person, filed by, type, start, end, start half, end half, days, status (`pending`, `approved`, `rejected`, `cancelled`, `withdrawn`), replacement and its answer, document attachment, process instance, comment, timestamps. Days are kept per date in `leave_request_day` (date, fraction, leave year) so a recount and a team view read the same days.

- Events `LeaveApproved(RequestId, UserId, From, To)` and `LeaveCancelled(RequestId, UserId, From, To)`; IDs and dates only.

## Implementation notes (slice 1)
- The accrual and carry-over job runs every hour per active tenant (`LeaveMaintenanceWorker`); each period writes once per person and type, so the extra runs write nothing.
- Leave is a module service (`hr.leave`, route `/leave`) in the services catalogue, shown only when the plan has `hr.leave`. Module service registrations now carry an optional capability key.
- The preview returns each problem as a key with the message in the caller's language, so the page shows it as is.
- An hour type takes one day per request, in quarter hours; against the day count it weighs 8 hours a day.
- Eligibility is by membership type only; gender and religion rules wait for a spec decision. A person needs no org unit to take leave; without one the tenant policy applies.
- Replacement asks are personal tasks (`leave_replacement`) besides the request page. Cancelling or shortening approved leave starts a second approval (`leave_change`) when the type says so.
- HR adjustments have their API and audit; their page on /admin/leave came with slice 2 (pick a person, see the ledger, add or remove days with a reason).
- People profile "out of office" reads `ILeave.AvailabilityAsync`; wiring it into the profile is left to spec 010's next change.

## Slice 2: HR balances, recount and reduced hours
- Data: `hr.calendar_period` (name in each language, start, end, hours a day; at most 50 per calendar, under 100 days each, no overlap). `hr.leave_request` gains `hr_state` (queued, recorded, failed, unavailable), `hr_reference` and `hr_changes`. In the Integrations module: `integrations.hr_leave_setting` (source, allow stale submit, version), `integrations.hr_leave_balance` (last known balance per person and type, with the time read) and `integrations.hr_leave_handoff` (one row per request and change).
- Contracts: `WorkingCalendarSnapshot.ReducedHours(date)` and `Periods`; `IHrLeave` in `Integrations.Contracts` (setting, balances, hand-off) and the `HrLeaveHandOffChanged` event.
- API: `PUT /api/v1/hr/calendars/{id}/periods` (If-Match); `GET` and `PUT /api/v1/hr/integration/leave-source` (If-Match, `hr.integration_manage`, plan feature `hr.integration`); `GET /api/v1/hr/integration/leave-handoffs` and `POST .../leave-handoffs/{id}:retry`. The working days preview gives each day's reduced hours. Balances and the preview say whether the balance came from HR and when it was read.
- HR as the source: the HR REST pack reads one person's balances (HR codes mapped back to TeamBeats type keys) and takes approved leave, cancellations and changes as a POST, with a stable idempotency header per change. HR's balance is one figure per type; TeamBeats takes off pending requests and approved ones HR has not recorded. While HR owns balances the ledger is not written, accrual stops and adjustments are refused. The last known balance is kept with its time; when HR is down a request goes in on it only if the tenant allows that. Smallest safe default: it does not.
- Hand-offs are durable connector jobs. HR down or no usable connection: tried again every hour for three days from the hand-off, then failed. A refusal fails at once. Failed hand-offs show on the HR integration page and an HR administrator sends one again (audited `hr.leave_handoff_retried`). A cancellation or change goes to HR only for leave HR was told about.
- Recount (FR-008): every `WorkingCalendarChanged` recounts open day-unit requests that touch the changed dates, or, for a week or assignment change, those still to end for the people concerned (everyone for the tenant default). Changed days replace the request days; approved leave gets a `recount` ledger row per leave year for the difference (or a change hand-off when HR owns balances), is audited `hr.leave_recounted` and the person is told. At most 2000 requests per change.
- Reduced hours apply to working days only; attendance (slice 6) reads them. Leave counts are by day, so reduced hours do not change a leave count.
- Tests: periods (validation, version, snapshot, preview, permission), recount (holiday added and removed, ledger, pending request, notification), HR source (live balance, stale balance and the setting, not matched, hand-off recorded, cancelled, refused and sent again, three day expiry, no ledger rows, adjustment and accrual refused, permission, plan and other tenant).

## Slice 3: Journeys (T018, T019)
- Data: `hr.journey_template` (key, kind onboarding, offboarding, transfer or promotion, names, optional org unit whose subtree it serves, start trigger, steps as validated JSON, version), `hr.journey` (template and version, person, buddy, unit, anchor date, status active, completed or cancelled, the event that started it) with one active journey per template and person, and `hr.journey_step` (key, name, offset in days from the anchor, assignee, due working days, scheduled date, status scheduled, open, done or cancelled, the engine instance).
- Starts: `EmployeeHired` and `EmployeeRehired` (spec 019) start onboarding from a template started by hires, with the HR start date as the anchor; a hire with no account yet waits for the account. `UserProvisioned` (spec 003) starts onboarding from a template started by new accounts (anchored on the HR hire date, else today) and, when HR knows the hire date, from one started by hires. A new account whose anchor is more than 30 days ago starts nothing, so a directory's first sync of existing staff starts no journeys (smallest safe default; the spec is silent). `EmployeeTerminated` starts offboarding with the last working day; an approved request of the service a template names (spec 016 `RequestCompleted`, read through a new `IRequestFacts` contract of Services) starts offboarding with the date in the form field the template names. `UserDeprovisioned` starts nothing: the account is disabled at the end of offboarding (US5 scenario 3). A template applies when its unit is the person's primary unit or an ancestor; the nearest wins, and only one active template per start, unit and service is allowed. A repeated event changes nothing (unique per template and source event); an event for a person who already has an active journey of that template moves its anchor, so every step not yet open is rescheduled (US4 scenario 2). A re-hire's journey links the earlier one.
- Run on spec 024: each template version publishes one single step process per step, `hr.journey.<template>.<version>.<step>` (source `journey_route`), so a journey keeps the routes of the version it started with. The journey worker (every two minutes, `Hr:Journeys:InProcess` in the development host) and every start or move open steps whose date has come, business key journey and step. Assignees: the person or the buddy (a new `CandidateRule.Subject` flag: the instance's subject, given as `requestedFor`), the line manager, named people, or holders of a permission at the person's unit (a queue). Person, buddy and manager steps fall back to the step's permission, `hr.manage` when not given, so a step of a person who left, or a buddy step with no buddy, goes to HR. The person is excluded from every step that is not theirs. Due working days give reminders and overdue escalation as for any task, and `JourneyStepOverdue` is published. Approve marks the step done, reject or return marks it not needed; the journey completes when no step is left. Tasks show in the inbox through an `ITaskSource` with the journey link, and the assignee is notified (queue steps are found in the inbox).
- Cancel: HR cancels a journey with a reason, which withdraws open steps and cancels the rest (audited). The engine lets the user a module authorized withdraw an instance the system started. A no-show cancels onboarding and, when an offboarding template started by termination (else a manual one) applies, starts it anchored today (edge case). HR also moves the anchor and names the buddy.
- Dashboard and pages: HR officers covering the person's unit see journeys with progress, open and overdue steps on `/admin/journeys`, next to the templates. `/hr/journeys/{id}` shows the steps to the person, the buddy, anyone with a task in it and HR; a waiting step is marked done there. `/hr/journeys` lists the person's own journeys. Audit: `hr.journey_template_changed`, `hr.journey_started`, `hr.journey_rescheduled`, `hr.journey_step_completed`, `hr.journey_cancelled`.
- T019 step types: a template step has a type, `task` (the default), `attestation`, `access_removal` or `exit_interview`. Every typed step still runs on the engine, so due dates, overdue escalation and `JourneyStepOverdue` work as for any task.
  - Attestation (US4 scenario 4, FR-015 with spec 025 FR-017): the person does it, and only the person. Spec 025 gets an "onboarding required" flag on the register entry and `policies.obligation`, the person's own attestation task outside any campaign, asked for through a new `IPolicyObligations` contract with the journey step as the source. When the step opens, every published flagged policy that applies to the person and whose version in force they have not attested to becomes an obligation due with the step (five working days when the step has none). The obligations show in the inbox and on My policies like campaign attestations, so the step's own task stays out of the inbox and cannot be decided. One attestation to the version in force settles every obligation for the policy. `PolicyAttested` and the worker close the step when nothing is left (a retired policy no longer counts); with nothing to attest to the step closes as not needed; a cancelled journey cancels the obligations.
  - Access removal (US5 scenario 4, FR-017): never done by the person or the buddy. When the step opens it gets one line per enabled connection that is not disabled (a new `IConnectedSystems` contract of Integrations) and one per system the template names (up to 20, for systems that are not connected). Whoever holds the task confirms each line as removed or not applicable, with an optional note (audited `hr.access_removal_confirmed`); the last line completes the task and the step. A decision from the inbox while a line is pending is refused. Lines still pending once the first working day after the step opened has ended (the person's unit calendar) go once to the tenant holders of `security.manage` and `integration.operate` (`hr.access_removal_escalated`, audited).
  - Exit interview (US5 scenario 5, FR-018): offboarding only, by the leaver or whoever the step names (HR officers through the fallback). The form records one to five reasons from a fixed list, an optional main reason, a rating from 1 to 5, whether they would come back and a comment of up to 2,000 characters, or that the person declined (the step then closes as not needed). The comment reaches HR officers covering the person only; the leaver and whoever recorded it see the structured answers; the audit log records that a comment exists, never its text. `GET /api/v1/hr/exit-reasons` counts reasons for the units the officer covers over a period (twelve months by default, at most three years), with the minimum group size of spec 021 (5): smaller units are merged into "other", and when that is still too small it is left out and counted as hidden. Spec 021 dashboards can read these counts when they are built.
- T019 pre-boarding (US4 scenario 3, FR-016), behind the tenant setting `hr.journey_settings.preboarding_enabled` (versioned, off by default; read by any HR officer, changed only by one covering the whole tenant, audited `hr.journey_settings_changed`; turning it off cancels invitations not sent yet).
  - A template step of type `preboarding` belongs to an onboarding template started by a hire, is the person's own, comes before day one (negative offset) and carries a welcome text per language (plain text, up to 2,000 characters). A joiner with an account reads it as their own step in the journey.
  - Spec 019 gets an optional canonical field `personal_email` (checked like the work email). It is kept out of `HrRecordView`, so profiles, prefill and every other module never see it; `IHrRecords.PreHireAsync` returns it only for an active record matched to no account, with the start date and the unit its org code maps to.
  - `EmployeeHired` or `EmployeeRehired` without an account schedules one invitation per HR record (`hr.preboarding`) for the earliest pre-boarding step's date (today when that has passed) of the onboarding template that applies to the mapped unit. A later hire event moves it. The address is never stored in the Hr schema: the journey worker reads it from HR when the email goes, and cancels the invitation when the hire got an account, left, lost the personal email, has started, or the template lost its pre-boarding steps.
  - The email goes through a new Notifications contract `IExternalEmail` (template `hr.preboarding_invite`, tenant default language, tenant relay) with a link `/preboarding/{token}`: 32 random bytes, of which only the SHA-256 hash is kept. A relay failure retries every 15 minutes up to 12 times; a bounce ends it. The link works until the end of the start date in the tenant's time zone, and never more than 30 days after it was sent. Audit `hr.preboarding_sent` names the HR record, never the address.
  - `GET /api/v1/preboarding/{token}` needs no session; the tenant comes from the host, as for inbound webhooks. It returns the start date, when the link ends and the pre-boarding steps (names, text, date), nothing else, and records the first opening. Unknown, ended, expired and other tenants' links are all not found. The web page `/preboarding/{token}` sends no referrer and is not indexed. `/admin/journeys` shows the setting and how many invitations wait, went out, were opened or failed (counts only).
- Learning assignments (FR-015 second half) wait for spec 028; a template step can be a manual task until then.

## Slice 4: Attendance (T020)
- T020 part 1 (US3 scenarios 1, 2 and 5, FR-010 first half, FR-012):
  - Data: `hr.attendance_settings` (versioned tenant setting: web sign in, off by default; location mode `off`, `flag` or `refuse`; up to 20 named sites with a centre and a radius of 10 m to 5 km), `hr.work_rule` (start, end and grace minutes for late arrival and early leave, one for the whole tenant and at most one per unit, versioned), `hr.attendance_punch` (time, direction, source web or clock, remote flag; clock punches unique per connection and punch ID) and `hr.attendance_import` (how far clock data has been read).
  - Sign in: `POST /api/v1/me/attendance/punches` with a direction, and with the browser's position when the location check is on. The API compares it with the sites; outside every site the punch is marked remote (`flag`) or refused (`refuse`). Only the remote flag is kept, never the coordinates (section 8 "location if enabled", smallest safe default). Two punches less than a minute apart are refused as a double tap. Web sign in off means attendance comes only from the time system.
  - Clock data: `ClockDataImported` (spec 017) makes the Hr handler read new punches through `IClockData` from the last sequence read, so a late or repeated import adds each punch once (edge case). A punch with no direction is paired by time: the opposite of the person's punch before it on the same local day.
  - Days: computed on read from the punches, the rule of the nearest unit (tenant rule, else 09:00 to 17:00 with 15 minutes of grace, smallest safe default), the person's working calendar (holidays, half days, reduced hours periods: the day then ends that many hours after the start, US2 scenario 3) and approved leave. A day is a working day, day off, holiday or leave, and shows first in, last out, hours worked (paired in and out only), expected hours and the exceptions `late`, `early_leave`, `missing_punch` (an out with no in, two ins, or an in still open once the day is over), `absent` (a past full working day with no punch), `remote` and `worked_day_off` (work on a holiday or day off, which part 2 turns into a compensation request). Approved leave removes the day's exceptions, and a half day halves the expected hours with no late or early check.
  - Who sees what: the person sees their own month (`GET /api/v1/me/attendance?month=yyyy-mm`); anyone in their manager chain and HR officers covering their unit read `GET /api/v1/hr/attendance/people/{id}`; everyone else gets not found. `GET /api/v1/hr/attendance/team` gives each direct report's totals. HR officers read and change the settings (whole tenant only) and the rules of the units they cover on `/admin/attendance`, and export a month as CSV for payroll (`GET /api/v1/hr/attendance/export`, at most 5,000 people, with a leading formula character neutralised). Audit: `hr.attendance_settings_changed`, `hr.work_rule_changed`, `hr.attendance_exported`.
  - Pages: `/attendance` (sign in and out, the month), `/attendance/team`, `/attendance/people/{id}` and `/admin/attendance`; the Attendance module service joins the catalogue where the plan has `hr.attendance`.
- T020 part 2 (US3 scenarios 3 and 4, FR-010 second half, FR-011):
  - Data: `hr.attendance_request` (kind `correction` or `compensation`, the day, a reason of up to 500 characters, the punches to add or the days to credit and their leave type, status pending, approved, rejected or withdrawn, the engine instance); punches added by a correction keep the request (`source` `correction`); the leave ledger gets the reason `compensation`; the attendance settings get the leave type compensation days go to (none means compensation requests are off).
  - Requests: the person asks for one of their own days from the last 60 days up to today (smallest safe default; the spec is silent). A correction adds one to four times, each a sign in or sign out, none later than now; one correction per day may wait at a time. A compensation is for a day showing `worked_day_off`: a full day for at least the rule's day length, half a day for at least half of it, nothing for less (smallest safe default); one per day, and refused when HR keeps balances. `POST /api/v1/me/attendance/requests`, `GET /api/v1/me/attendance/requests`, `GET /api/v1/hr/attendance/requests/{id}` (the person, the task holder, the manager chain and HR officers covering them; else not found) and `POST .../{id}:withdraw` by the person while it waits.
  - Approval: one line manager step on spec 024 (`hr.attendance`, source `attendance_route`), HR officers of the unit when there is no manager; the person is never a candidate. The task shows in the inbox (source `attendance`) and the manager is notified. Approval of a correction adds its punches, marks the day `corrected`, publishes `AttendanceCorrected` and audits `hr.attendance_corrected` with the day's punches before and after and the approver; approval of a compensation writes a `compensation` ledger row in the leave year of the day (audit `hr.compensation_credited`). A rejection changes nothing (audit `hr.attendance_request_rejected`). The person is notified either way.
  - Pages: the request form and my requests on `/attendance`, `/attendance/requests/{id}` with the decision panel for the manager, and the compensation leave type on `/admin/attendance`.

## Slice 5: Letters and data change (T021, T022)
- T021 letters (US7, FR-021, FR-022):
  - Data: `hr.letter_settings` (the public verification page, on by default, versioned; only HR officers of the whole tenant change it), `hr.letter_type` (a key, names and a plain text template in English and Arabic, up to 10 manual fields HR types in, whether HR approves, the whole tenant or one unit subtree, active, versioned; up to 50 types; a type with manual fields always needs approval), `hr.letter_counter` and `hr.letter` (reference `LT-000001` numbered per tenant, the person, type, language, addressee, the values HR typed in, status pending, approved, issued, rejected or withdrawn, the engine instance, the SHA-256 of the verification code, the company name, issue date and PDF object key, and the type's names and version as issued).
  - Templates: plain text with `{placeholder}` names checked on save: `name` (Arabic or English from HR, else the directory name), `employee_number`, `job_title`, `grade`, `hire_date` (from the matched HR record, spec 019), `company` (tenant display name), `addressee` (else "To whom it may concern" in the letter's language), `date` (the issue date), `reference` and the type's manual fields. A blank line starts a paragraph. Every value and line is HTML encoded, so neither a template nor an HR value can become markup.
  - Asking: the person chooses an active type of the tenant or of a unit on their path, English or Arabic and an optional addressee of up to 200 characters; at most 5 letters wait at once (smallest safe default). A type without approval is issued at once (SC-004) and refused with `conflict.letters.data_missing` when HR lacks a value it needs. A type with approval runs one step on spec 024 (`hr.letter`, source `letter_route`) for HR officers covering the person, who is never a candidate. The holder of the task sees what the letter will say and types in manual fields and missing HR values (`PUT /api/v1/hr/letters/{id}/values`, plain text of up to 200 characters); approving with a value still missing is refused and the task stays open.
  - Issuing: the filled text is printed through Gotenberg's Chromium route (`IDocumentConverter.HtmlToPdfAsync`, A4) with the company at the top and the reference, the verification code and the verification address at the foot; Arabic letters run right to left and use Arabic-Indic digits when the tenant's numbering system is `arab`. The PDF is kept in tenant object storage, the typed values are cleared and the letter is issued with `LetterIssued` and audit `hr.letter_issued`; a later template change never changes it. When the converter is down the letter stays approved and the journeys worker retries it every two minutes, up to 20 times. The code is 12 characters from an alphabet without look-alikes (about 59 bits), printed as three groups of four; only its hash is kept.
  - Verification: `GET /api/v1/letters/verify/{code}` on the tenant host without sign in, limited to 30 calls a minute per address, returns only the reference, type names, issue date and company (FR-022) and audits `hr.letter_verified`. An unknown code, a code of another tenant, the platform host, a turned off page or a plan without `hr.letters` all give not found.
  - Reading: `GET /api/v1/me/letters` and `/types`, `POST /api/v1/me/letters`, `GET /api/v1/hr/letters` (HR covering the person), `GET /api/v1/hr/letters/{id}` (the person, the task holder and HR covering the person; else not found), `POST .../{id}:withdraw` while it waits, `GET .../{id}/pdf` for the person and HR covering them, `GET`/`PUT /api/v1/hr/letter-types[/{key}]` and `/api/v1/hr/letter-settings` with `If-Match`. Audit also `hr.letter_type_changed`, `hr.letter_settings_changed`, `hr.letter_requested`, `hr.letter_rejected`.
  - Pages: `/letters` (ask and my letters), `/letters/{id}` (download, withdraw, and for HR the values and the decision panel), the public `/letters/verify` and `/admin/letters`; the HR letters module service joins the catalogue where the plan has `hr.letters`.

- T022 data change (US6, FR-019, FR-020):
  - Groups and checks: `address` (address line, city, country, optional postal code), `emergency_contact` (name, relationship, phone), `bank` (bank name, account holder, IBAN checked with ISO 13616 mod 97 and kept upper case without spaces), `marital_status` (single, married, divorced or widowed) and `dependants` (a count from 0 to 20). Unknown fields, missing required ones, control characters and long values are refused per field (`values.{key}`). Bank details are sensitive; bank, marital status and dependants need a supporting document by default.
  - Data: `hr.data_change_settings` (per group offered or not and document or not, versioned; only HR officers of the whole tenant change it, with `If-Match`), `hr.data_change_document` (PDF, PNG or JPEG up to 10 MB in tenant object storage, only the uploader may attach it, soft deleted), `hr.data_change_request` (group, field keys, the values while the change is open, reason, document, status pending, approved, completed, write_failed, rejected or withdrawn, write back `hr` or `manual`, HR reference, engine instance). One open change per person and group.
  - Flow: a change runs one step on spec 024 (`hr.data_change`, source `data_change_route`) for HR officers covering the person, who is never a candidate. On approval it goes to the HR system through `IHrDataChange` when the HR REST connection has a data change address (spec 019); the hand-off POSTs `request_id`, `record_id`, `employee_number`, `group` and `values` with the idempotency header `teambeats-data-change-{id}`, retries hourly for three days and answers with `HrDataChangeHandOffChanged`. Without such a connection the change is approved with write back `manual`, and HR enters it in the HR system and marks it done (`:complete`).
  - Keeping nothing (FR-020 and the privacy rules): the values are cleared when the change is completed, failed, refused or withdrawn; the hand-off input and the connector run input are cleared as soon as the HR system answers; payload capture never keeps `submit_data_change`; audit records only group and field keys. The document is deleted at every end for sensitive groups and when a change is refused or withdrawn. The profile keeps HR's value until the next sync, since TeamBeats never writes the values to the profile (US6 scenario 2).
  - Notices: a sensitive change sends a mandatory security notice on every channel when it is asked for and when it ends; other groups notify through the person's preferences.
  - API: `GET`/`POST /api/v1/me/data-changes`, `/groups`, `POST /documents?name=`, `GET /api/v1/hr/data-changes` (HR covering the person), `GET .../{id}` and `/{id}/document` (the person, the task holder and HR covering them; else not found), `POST .../{id}:withdraw` (the person while it waits), `POST .../{id}:complete` (HR covering the person, manual write back only), `GET`/`PUT /api/v1/hr/data-change-settings`. All need `hr.data_change`. Audit `hr.data_change_settings_changed`, `_requested`, `_approved`, `_rejected`, `_written_back`, `_write_failed`.
  - Pages: `/my-details` (ask and my changes), `/my-details/requests/{id}` (values, document, withdraw, mark done and the decision panel) and `/admin/data-changes`; the HR connection form gets the data change address, reference path and idempotency header.

## Slice 6: Expenses (T023)
- Claims (US8 scenarios 1, 3 to 6, FR-023, FR-025):
  - Roles: `finance.process` and the built-in Finance Officer role (spec 004 FR-011) with it, org view and people view; tenant administrators get it too. Finance officers of the whole tenant set the policy and the rates; finance officers covering a person see and decide the person's claims.
  - Data: `hr.expense_policy` (versioned, `If-Match`): the policy currency (no claim can be sent until it is set), the window in days (60 by default, section 6), up to 30 categories (`amount` lines from a receipt in any currency, `per_diem` lines of days at the category rate, `mileage` lines of kilometres at the category rate, whether a receipt is needed, limits per grade and one for every grade), up to 3 amount tiers with 1 to 10 approvers each, and an optional list of cost centres. The default policy has five receipt categories and no currency. `hr.expense_rate` (currency, from date, rate in the policy currency): the rate source is this table kept by finance, the smallest safe default, and a rate feed through spec 017 can fill it later. `hr.expense_receipt` (PDF, PNG or JPEG up to 10 MB with its SHA-256, scanned, only the uploader attaches it), `hr.expense_claim` (reference `EX-000001` per tenant, title, cost centre, project, total in the policy currency, duplicate and over limit flags, status, finance state, reference and payment status) and `hr.expense_line` (amount and currency as spent, the rate, the amount in the policy currency, the limit, the justification, the receipt and the duplicate flag).
  - Checks per line: a known category, a date not in the future and inside the window, an amount above zero with two decimals or days from 1 to 31 or up to 5,000 km, a three letter currency with a rate on or before the expense date, tax not above the amount, plain text, and a receipt where the category asks for one. A line above the limit for the person's grade (from HR when spec 019 holds the grade) needs a justification of up to 500 characters. At most 50 lines and 20 waiting claims per person.
  - Duplicates (US8 scenario 4, section 13): a line with the same merchant (ignoring case), date, amount and currency as another live line of the person, or a receipt with the same SHA-256 as one on another live claim of the tenant, is flagged on the line and the claim for the approver; it is never refused.
  - Route (scenario 5): the line manager (finance covering the person when there is none, or when the manager is the claimant), then one step per tier the total is above with that tier's approvers, then finance covering the person. Each shape is a definition `hr.expense.{hash}` (source `expense_route`), so claims with the same steps share one; nobody decides their own claim.
  - Hand-off (scenario 6): once finance approves, `ExpenseApproved` is published and the claim goes to the finance or ERP system through `IFinanceHandOff` (spec 017) with the employee number from HR; the finance reference and payment status come back through `FinanceHandOffChanged`, and a paid claim notifies the person. Without a finance connection or an employee number the claim stays approved and finance pays it outside TeamBeats.
  - API: `GET /api/v1/me/expenses`, `/form`, `POST /api/v1/me/expenses`, `POST /receipts?name=`, `GET /api/v1/hr/expenses` (finance covering the person), `GET .../{id}` and `/{id}/receipts/{receiptId}` (the person, the task holder and finance covering them; else not found), `POST .../{id}:withdraw` (the person while it waits), `GET`/`PUT /api/v1/hr/expense-policy` and `/api/v1/hr/expense-rates`. All need `hr.expenses`. Audit `hr.expense_policy_changed`, `hr.expense_rates_changed`, `hr.expense_submitted`, `hr.expense_approved`, `hr.expense_rejected`, `hr.expense_handed_off`.
  - Pages: `/expenses` (send a claim and my claims), `/expenses/{id}` (both amounts, limits, flags, receipts, withdraw and the decision panel) and `/admin/expenses` (the policy, the rates and the claims). The policy is edited as validated JSON for now; a form for categories and tiers is a follow-up.
- AI receipt reading (FR-024): `POST /api/v1/me/expenses/receipts/{id}:read` needs `ai.receipt_reading` and the caller's own clean receipt that is not on a claim yet. The file goes to the AI gateway as an attachment of a Confidential request with a JSON schema for merchant, date, amount, currency and tax, so the tenant AI policy decides whether any model may read it; under the default policy the answer is `policy_blocked` and nothing is sent. Values that cannot be right (a future date, a negative amount, a tax above the amount, a currency that is not three letters) are dropped. The answer is a suggestion only: nothing is stored, and the page copies values into the line only when the person chooses "Use these values".
- The gateway contract gains message attachments (PNG, JPEG or PDF, at most 10 MB). A request with attachments needs a model with the `vision` capability, counts 1,500 tokens per file in the context check, and the debug window never keeps the file.
- Spec 013 has a fixed list of use cases and none for receipts, so receipt reading uses `summarize` (smallest safe default). Its route and Confidential allowance are the tenant's to set.
